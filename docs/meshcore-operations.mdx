# MeshCore Operations

These operator checklists focus on fixed MeshCore infrastructure for New
England: Repeaters, Room Servers, and optional MQTT observer setups.

## Understand MeshCore roles

MeshCore **Companion** devices are personal or local nodes. They are commonly
carried, used at home with a computer, or installed in a car. Most communicate
with the MeshCore app over Bluetooth or USB; some use Wi-Fi.

MeshCore **Repeaters** and **Room Servers** are the infrastructure devices to
plan carefully. They should be stable, reachable, and documented because other
operators may depend on them for coverage and routing.

## Regional radio presets

**Use the US preset for most of New England.** Central Vermont also has an
established MeshCore network using the coordinated 500 kHz alternative below.
Check with local operators before choosing a preset, especially when traveling
in Vermont.

| Setting | US preset — most of New England | Central Vermont / coordinated 500 kHz alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | 910.525 MHz | 910.525 MHz |
| Bandwidth | 62.5 kHz | 500 kHz |
| Spreading factor | 7 | 12 |
| Coding rate | 5 | 6 |

This is **not a request to move existing networks to 500 kHz**. If your local
community chooses to test 500 kHz elsewhere in New England, coordinate with
nearby operators and use the same alternative rather than creating a competing
500 kHz preset.

**The two presets do not communicate directly over the air.** Do not assume a
bridge exists between them; changing presets can disconnect you from nearby
repeaters. MeshCore forwarding regions do not bridge different radio presets.

The [RUT Vermont MeshMapper map](https://rut.meshmapper.net/) is a map-service
identifier, separate from the `vt` forwarding region on our [boundary
map](/regions). Neither label guarantees that every local node uses the same
preset. Confirm local settings rather than applying the Central Vermont preset
to all of Vermont or assuming it applies to `ct-rv`.

## Set up MeshCore Repeaters and Room Servers

1. Pick a legal site with good elevation, weather protection, stable power, and
   safe antenna mounting.
2. Use supported MeshCore hardware and record the device, antenna, power source,
   maintainer, and install location.
3. Flash the current stable MeshCore firmware and record the version.
4. Configure the correct MeshCore role for the site: Repeater or Room Server.
5. Test locally before installing at height.
6. After deployment, verify coverage from multiple directions and watch for
   channel congestion.

Useful links:

- [MeshCore docs](https://docs.meshcore.io/)
- [MeshCore flasher](https://flasher.meshcore.io/)
- [MeshCore map](https://map.meshcore.io/)
- [CT Mesh repeater setup](https://ctmesh.org/repeater-setup) — hardware,
  flashing, USB configuration, companion-based management, and deployment.
  Follow CT-specific region settings only for Connecticut sites; use our
  [region map](/regions) and local guidance elsewhere.

## Publish observer data

An observer reports the local MeshCore traffic it hears to MQTT so maps and
analysis tools can show network activity. Choose either dedicated observer
firmware on a compatible Wi-Fi node or a radio connected to a separate Linux
host. Reporting to MQTT does not itself extend RF coverage.

You do not need to pair every Repeater or Room Server with an observer. New
England Mesh already has many observers on the main mesh, so add another one
when it improves regional visibility or fills a known monitoring gap.

### Dedicated Observer Firmware — recommended for compatible Wi-Fi nodes

[CT Mesh recommends dedicated Observer Firmware](https://ctmesh.org/observer-setup)
for most Wi-Fi-connected fixed observers. It runs directly on the radio,
publishes to community MQTT services, and synchronizes the node's clock through
NTP without a separate always-on computer.

Use the [Observer Firmware flasher](https://observer.gessaman.com/?config=mqtt-observer)
and [firmware setup guide](https://observer.gessaman.com/docs.html). Select the
build for your exact hardware and revision, match the [local radio
preset](#regional-radio-presets), and follow your community's broker and region
instructions. Do not copy another region's example settings unchanged.

For Connecticut, follow the [CT Mesh observer setup](https://ctmesh.org/observer-setup)
for its `ctmesh` broker preset, `BDL` reporting identifier, and verification
steps. These are CT-specific settings, not defaults for every New England
observer.

### Linux / mctomqtt — alternative for a separate host

Use [meshcoretomqtt (`mctomqtt`)](https://github.com/Cisien/meshcoretomqtt) when
you already operate a Raspberry Pi/Linux host or your radio has no suitable
network connection.

1. Follow the project guide to select suitable packet-logging firmware and
   connect the radio to the host over USB serial.
2. Install and configure `mctomqtt`, including your community's MQTT destination
   and observer reporting identifier.
3. Start the service, confirm MQTT connectivity, and verify that observations
   appear in the intended map or analyzer.
4. Document the radio, host, antenna, power source, and maintainer.

CT Mesh's [observer guide](https://ctmesh.org/observer-setup) includes this Linux
method as well as dedicated firmware. It also explains when a nearby observer
should stop repeating to avoid redundant RF forwarding.

## Community MQTT services

New England Mesh operates a combined MQTT backend for Connecticut,
Massachusetts, and New Hampshire data used by the [regional
map](https://map.newenglandme.sh/) and
[analyzer](https://analyzer.newenglandme.sh/). That backend does **not** accept
direct observer uploads. Operators should publish to their local community's
MQTT service; the regional services aggregate that community data upstream.

MQTT infrastructure is operated by the individual regional communities, not by
New England Mesh. Follow the community's current instructions and access policy
before connecting an observer:

- **Greater Boston Mesh:** [`mqttmc01.bostonme.sh` setup and broker
  details](https://bostonme.sh/docs/MeshCore/meshcore-mqtt). The documented
  listener uses secure WebSockets on port `443` with token authentication.
- **Connecticut Mesh:** [Repeater setup](https://ctmesh.org/repeater-setup) and
  [observer setup](https://ctmesh.org/observer-setup), including dedicated
  firmware and Linux instructions. Its uplink-only broker at `mqtt.ctmesh.org`
  uses the `BDL` reporting identifier; MQTT reporting does not bridge RF networks.
- **NHMesh:** [NHMesh knowledge base](https://nhmesh.live/kb). Use its current
  Observer / Gateway Setup and MQTT guidance for New Hampshire deployments.
- **Maine Mesh:** [Maine Mesh observer
  configuration](https://github.com/deejdeej/MaineMesh-Meshcore/blob/main/observer/README.md).
  Maine has a [combined multi-region MeshMapper map](https://maine.meshmapper.net/),
  with [BGR — Maine Statewide](https://bgr.meshmapper.net/) and
  [RKD — Knox County](https://rkd.meshmapper.net/). The linked observer guide
  provides a `BGR` example; that is not Maine's only MeshMapper/IATA code.
  Confirm the appropriate code with local operators, especially for Knox County.
  MeshMapper/IATA identifiers are separate from the `me` MeshCore coordination
  region. The documented observer example publishes to the Greater Boston Mesh
  broker in addition to its other monitoring destinations.

Broker credentials and connection requirements can change. Use the linked
community documentation rather than copying credentials into this site.
